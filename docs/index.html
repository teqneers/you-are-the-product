Why this matters

What you see

Click the icon. See who is there.

The popup shows the current page, the side panel keeps it in view, and the overview shows everything at once.

  • Who receives what: the page you read, IDs to recognise you, the same ID sent to several companies
  • "Sold." Ad auctions on the page and every bidder that got your visit
  • Your consent in plain words: how many companies you agreed to
  • Ad pixels decoded: products, prices, search terms, hashed email
  • Email leaks from forms, even before you press Submit
  • Session replay: watch your own mouse movements the way they see them
  • Fingerprinting and trackers disguised as part of the site
  • Data brokers among the companies watching
  • 14 languages

For teachers

A 10-minute exercise

  1. Guess: how many companies watch when you open a news site?
  2. Accept: open a private window, a big news site, click "Accept all". Count.
  3. Reject: new private window, same site, click "Reject". Count again.
  4. Talk: why is "Accept" easier to find? Who pays for the site?

Full instructions in the description on GitHub.

Your privacy

A tracker watcher must not track you

  • Everything stays in your browser. No server, no account, no analytics.
  • It blocks nothing. It shows what normally happens. To protect yourself, use a blocker such as uBlock Origin, then watch the counter drop.
  • Open source under the GPL, so anyone can check.
